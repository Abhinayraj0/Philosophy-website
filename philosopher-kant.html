Immanuel Kant

Kant is a central figure of the Enlightenment whose philosophy changed the focus of inquiry from the world itself to the human mind that perceives it.

Era: 1724–1804 • Main themes: Knowledge, Ethics, Reason

Introduction

Immanuel Kant spent his life in Königsberg, where his punctual daily routine became famous among locals. Despite never traveling far, his ideas reshaped global philosophy.

Core Philosophy

Kant argued that knowledge requires both rational thought and sensory experience. In the Critique of Pure Reason, he showed the mind actively structures reality through space, time, and causality.

Ethics

His Categorical Imperative holds that we should act only on principles we would want everyone to follow, making duty the basis of moral action.

Impact

Kant shifted philosophy from asking what the universe is to asking what we can know about the universe. His moral framework still guides contemporary debates in human rights and ethics.

Notable Quotes

“Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law.”

“Dare to know! Have the courage to use your own understanding.”
