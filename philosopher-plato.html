Plato

Plato was Socrates’ most devoted student, founder of the Academy, and architect of a metaphysical vision that still shapes Western thought.

Era: 428–348 BCE • Main themes: Forms, Politics, Knowledge

Introduction

Plato turned away from politics after Socrates’ execution and founded the Academy in Athens—widely considered the first higher learning institution in the Western world.

Core Philosophy

Plato proposed the Theory of Forms: the material world is changeable and imperfect, while the true reality is a timeless realm of perfect Forms, such as Justice, Beauty, and Equality.

Political Vision

In The Republic, he argued that the ideal society should be ruled by philosopher-kings who understand the Forms and can guide citizens wisely.

Impact

Plato’s ideas laid the groundwork for metaphysics, ethics, aesthetics, and political theory. Many historians say all Western philosophy is merely "a series of footnotes to Plato."

Notable Quotes

“The beginning is the most important part of the work.”

“Wise men speak because they have something to say; fools because they have to say something.”
