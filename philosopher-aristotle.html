Aristotle

If Plato was the great idealist, his star student Aristotle was the great pragmatist, building philosophy from observation and classification.

Era: 384–322 BCE • Main themes: Empiricism, Logic, Ethics

Introduction

Aristotle studied at Plato’s Academy for nearly twenty years before breaking away. He later tutored Alexander the Great and founded the Lyceum, where he pursued research across disciplines.

Core Philosophy

Aristotle believed that knowledge comes from observing the physical world, not from abstract forms. He classified living beings, political systems, and poetic genres with a rigor unmatched in his time.

Ethics

His concept of the Golden Mean proposed that virtue lies between extremes—courage is the middle way between cowardice and recklessness.

Impact

Aristotle wrote hundreds of treatises on topics from biology to poetry. His logic and classification systems dominated medieval scholarship and shaped both Christian and Islamic philosophy for over a millennium.

Notable Quotes

“It is the mark of an educated mind to be able to entertain a thought without accepting it.”

“The whole is greater than the sum of its parts.”
